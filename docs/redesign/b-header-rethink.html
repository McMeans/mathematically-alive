D1 · Goal ledger

Each goal is one row: name, status, number. The status word (amber with a lamp dot while live, muted once settled) replaces the tags, beside the number it describes. Numbers line up in one right-aligned column, so they compare at a glance, and the playoff road sits under its own row. The ledger is as tall as the identity block beside it, so nothing floats; an independent is one row. Reads like a standings table: dense and calm.

D2 · Goals across

The scoreboard version. Goals side by side with big lit numbers, each with its status word under it and the playoff road under Playoff. No fill, no band: it's as wide as the goals it has, so an independent is one unit beside its identity block. The loudest of the three; closest to what the header is today, minus the tags and the strip.

D3 · Verdict

Say it. One short sentence answers the page's question ("In the CUSA title game. Playoff: 17%, in the hunt."), with the key facts lit amber; the rest of the numbers sit under it as fine print. Most direct for a fan who just wants the answer, and naturally sized to any team. The sentence would come from the core (like the path text), so it's tested; the cost is fewer numbers at full size.

Common to all three: the record moves into the picker; tags become one status word per goal; Reset shows only with picks ("2 picks · Reset" in amber, which also tells the visitor the numbers include their picks); "Open picks in Lab" shortens to "Open in Lab". On phones the sticky name line would carry the Playoff number (and its status in D1/D2); the rest scrolls away as now.
